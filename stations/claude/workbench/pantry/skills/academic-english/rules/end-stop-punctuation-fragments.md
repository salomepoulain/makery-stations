# End-stop punctuation, fragments, and comma splices

## End-stop punctuation

Close every sentence with a period, question mark, or exclamation point. A sentence without one of these three is not finished, no matter how it looks on the page.

## Fragments

A fragment is a group of words punctuated as a sentence but missing a subject, a finite verb, or both, so it cannot stand on its own. Keep fragments out of academic prose.
- Fragment: *Because the sample size was too small.*
- Fixed: *Because the sample size was too small, the researchers discarded the trial.*

## Comma rules

**a. Comma before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses.** Put a comma before *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* when each side of it could stand as its own sentence.
- Correct: *The lab closed early, but the data kept syncing overnight.*
- Comma splice (incorrect): *The lab closed early, the data kept syncing overnight.*

**b. Comma after an introductory clause or phrase.** A subordinate clause or prepositional phrase that opens a sentence is followed by a comma.
- *Once the reviewers had approved the draft, the authors submitted it to the journal.*

**c. Commas between items in a series.** Separate three or more items in a list with commas.
- *The survey covered income, education, occupation, and household size.*

**d. Serial comma before the final conjunction.** Keep the comma before the last item in a series to prevent the last two items from reading as one unit.
- *The grant funded the interviewers, the transcriptionist, and the statistician.* (three separate roles, not two)

**e. Commas between stacked adjectives, not between an adverb and the adjective it modifies.** Coordinate adjectives that each independently describe the noun take a comma between them; an adverb feeding into the adjective that follows it does not.
- *The team reviewed the long, repetitive transcript.* / *The team reviewed the surprisingly long transcript.*

**f. Commas around parenthetical insertions.** An aside that interrupts the main clause is set off by a comma on each side.
- *The results, in short, contradicted the earlier study.*

**g. Comma before or after a direct address.** Set off the name of the person being spoken to.
- *Professor Lin, the committee has a question about your methodology.*

**h. Comma before a quoted statement, but not before a quoted clause introduced by "that."** A speech tag is separated from the quotation it introduces by a comma, unless the quotation is folded into the sentence with "that."
- *The author wrote, "The effect disappears at scale."* / *The author claimed that "the effect disappears at scale" under those conditions.*

**i. Commas around an appositive.** A phrase that renames or explains a nearby noun is set off by commas.
- *Dr. Alvarez, the study's lead author, presented the findings at the conference.*
