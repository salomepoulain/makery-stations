# Semicolon rules

Use a semicolon to join two independent clauses that are closely related, when no connecting word joins them.

The lab ran out of reagent; the experiment had to wait until Monday.
The bridge closed for repairs; commuters rerouted through the tunnel.
The committee could not reach consensus by Friday; they scheduled a second vote for the following week.

If you add a connecting word such as "and," "but," or "so," switch the semicolon to a comma.

The lab ran out of reagent, so the experiment had to wait until Monday.
The bridge closed for repairs, and commuters rerouted through the tunnel.
The committee could not reach consensus by Friday, so they scheduled a second vote for the following week.

A semicolon also joins two independent clauses linked by a conjunctive adverb (however, moreover, therefore, consequently, otherwise, nevertheless, thus, and similar words).

The lab ran out of reagent; consequently, the experiment had to wait until Monday.
The bridge closed for repairs; however, commuters rerouted through the tunnel.
The committee could not reach consensus by Friday; therefore, they scheduled a second vote for the following week.

You can drop the semicolon and write two separate sentences instead, keeping the conjunctive adverb at the start of the second one.

The lab ran out of reagent. Consequently, the experiment had to wait until Monday.
The bridge closed for repairs. However, commuters rerouted through the tunnel.
The committee could not reach consensus by Friday. Therefore, they scheduled a second vote for the following week.
