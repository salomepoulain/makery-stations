# Concision

Academic prose carries no dead weight. Five ways to cut it.

1. **Cut words that spell out the obvious.** If the reader can reconstruct a detail from context, stating it outright just adds length.
   - Wordy: *Consider a scenario in which a person is engaged in the process of attempting to memorize the sequence of steps required to bake bread.*
   - Concise: *Consider someone trying to memorize the steps for baking bread.*

2. **Cut filler determiners and modifiers.** Flag words: kind of, sort of, type of, really, basically, for all intents and purposes, definitely, actually, generally, individual, specific, particular.
   - Wordy: *Any specific kind of tea will work for me.*
   - Concise: *Any tea will work for me.*

3. **Cut repeated wording.** Don't pair a word with a near-synonym that says the same thing twice.
   - Wordy: *The lab director viewed the backup generator as an unnecessary extravagance.*
   - Concise: *The lab director viewed the backup generator as an extravagance.*

4. **Cut redundant or illogical pairs.** Redundant pairs: basic fundamentals, each individual, end result, final outcome, free gift, future plans, important essentials, past history, past memories, sudden crisis, terrible tragedy, true facts, unexpected surprise, various differences, very unique.
   - Drop one word from each pair.

5. **Cut redundant categories.** A specific word already names its category, so naming the category again is dead weight. Redundant: large in size, often times, of a bright color, heavy in weight, period in time, round in shape, at an early time, economics field, of cheap quality, honest in character, unusual in nature.
   - Wordy: *At that point in time, most shoppers wanted phones that were black in color and light in weight.*
   - Concise: *At that point, most shoppers wanted black, light phones.*
