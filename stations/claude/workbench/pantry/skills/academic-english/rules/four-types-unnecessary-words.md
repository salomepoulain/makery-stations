# Four types of unnecessary words and phrases

Four recurring patterns pad academic prose without adding meaning. Cut them.

## 1. Dummy subjects

Constructions like *there is, there are, there was, there were, it is, it was* push the real subject later in the sentence and force a weak verb to carry the load.

- Wordy: *There were several factors that contributed to the delay in shipping.*
- Concise: *Several factors delayed shipping.*

Find the noun doing the real work and make it the subject.

## 2. Nominalizations

Turning a verb or adjective into a noun (typically ending in *-ion, -ment, -ity, -ness*) buries the action inside a noun phrase, which then needs a second, weaker verb to prop it up.

- Wordy: *The implementation of the new policy resulted in confusion among staff.*
- Concise: *Implementing the new policy confused staff.*
- Convert back to the verb: *evaluation* → *evaluate*, *reduction* → *reduce*, *assessment* → *assess*

## 3. Infinitive phrases

*To + verb* constructions often stand in for a plain finite verb, adding words without adding meaning.

- Wordy: *Our goal was to increase revenue and to reduce costs.*
- Concise: *We increased revenue and reduced costs.*

## 4. Circumlocutions
