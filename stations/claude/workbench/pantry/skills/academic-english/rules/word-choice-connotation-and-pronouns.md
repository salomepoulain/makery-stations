# Connotation and pronoun reference

Two narrow failure modes that word-choice.md doesn't cover: picking a near-synonym that carries the wrong emotional charge, and leaving a pronoun pointing at more than one possible noun.

## Same denotation, different connotation

Words can share a dictionary definition and still push the reader toward a different judgment. Before you settle on a word, ask what attitude it smuggles in alongside the meaning.

Set 1, describing a manager who checks work closely:

- "The editor scrutinized every draft." (neutral: close, careful review)
- "The editor polished every draft." (positive: careful attention improves the work)
- "The editor picked apart every draft." (negative: excessive, faultfinding scrutiny)

Set 2, describing someone who spends a lot on other people:

- "He was a generous host." (positive: freely giving)
- "He was a lavish host." (neutral-to-positive: spends a lot, emphasis on scale)
- "He was a reckless host." (negative: spending without judgment)

Pick the version that matches your actual claim. If your data shows a manager caught more errors than peers did, "scrutinized" reports that; "picked apart" accuses them of something you haven't argued for.

## Ambiguous pronoun reference

A pronoun needs exactly one plausible antecedent. If two nouns of the same gender or number appear nearby, the reader can't tell which one "it," "her," or "they" points to, and has to stop and guess.

**Possessive pattern.** The pronoun could belong to either of two people mentioned.

- Ambiguous: "Maria called her sister after her car broke down." (whose car, Maria's or the sister's?)
- Clear: "Maria called her sister after Maria's car broke down."

**Action pattern.** The pronoun could refer to either of two things acted on.

- Ambiguous: "When the crane lowered the beam onto the platform, it buckled." (did the beam buckle or the platform?)
- Clear: "When the crane lowered the beam onto the platform, the platform buckled."

The fix is almost always the same: replace the pronoun with the noun it was supposed to stand in for. Don't restructure the whole sentence looking for a clever workaround; just name the thing.
