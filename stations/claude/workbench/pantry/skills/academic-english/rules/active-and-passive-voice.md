# Active and passive voice

In an active sentence the subject does the verb; in a passive sentence the subject receives it. Active names who acts. Passive shifts the spotlight onto what happened to something, and lets the actor stay offstage or disappear entirely.

## Default to active

Active sentences are shorter and easier to parse, and academic writing should credit the person who did the work when that person matters.

- bad: *A new sampling method was developed by Torres and Lin to reduce bias in the survey panel.*
- good: *Torres and Lin developed a new sampling method to reduce bias in the survey panel.*

- bad: *The correlation between rainfall and crop yield was examined in this section.*
- good: *This section examines the correlation between rainfall and crop yield.*

## Reach for passive in five situations

1. The result matters more than who produced it.
- bad (forces an actor into focus): *The committee finally approved the merger after eight months of hearings.*
- good: *After eight months of hearings, the merger was finally approved.*

2. You need to hold the same subject across several sentences instead of jumping between actors.
- bad: *Researchers collected the samples in March. A separate lab tested them in April. The results committee reviewed the findings in June.*
- good: *The samples were collected in March, tested in April, and reviewed in June.*

3. You want to soften or deflect blame by leaving the actor out.
- bad: *I lost the original consent forms.*
- good: *The original consent forms were lost.*

4. The actor is unknown, irrelevant, or too general to name.
- bad: *Someone repairs the bridge every decade.*
- good: *The bridge is repaired every decade.*

5. You want a formal, rule-like tone that states a policy rather than an actor's decision.
- bad: *We do not permit pets in the laboratory after 6 PM.*
- good: *Pets are not permitted in the laboratory after 6 PM.*

## Weigh both in method sections

Passive can fit when the procedure, not the person running it, is the point; active fits once you credit a specific contributor.

- passive, fine here: *Samples were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for ten minutes before analysis.*
- active, better here: *Reyes designed the centrifugation protocol used across all three trials.*
