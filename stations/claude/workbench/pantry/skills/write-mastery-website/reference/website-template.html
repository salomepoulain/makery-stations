The exercise types, in the order they appear

PPrior knowledge
Something you are assumed to know, made fluent before new material builds on it. Its exercises have their own numbers, in gold, and no letter.
OOrient
A concrete probe you can already solve with what you know. It comes before a Theory block, so that the general rule feels earned and does not arrive cold.
TTest
A gate-check right after the worked example: the same task with smaller numbers. It shows whether you are ready to move on, and it drills a little too.
DDrill
The bulk of the practice: one family of the same move with different numbers, so the skill gets fast before anything harder is asked.
VVisualize
Predict or sketch a picture, such as how a curve moves when a parameter changes, on empty axes first. Then compare with the plot in the solution. It builds a feel for what the formula does.
EExam
The level the course expects: chained questions with no hints, where you choose the method and write out the whole argument. If you can do these cleanly, you are ready.
CChallenge
Harder than the exam asks, for example an unusual framing or an edge case. It is for depth, so skip it on a first pass.
RReflect
A short question that asks why. It revisits a step and generalizes it, answered in words. It deepens understanding, but the exam does not depend on it.

Short on time? Do T, D, V and E. C and R are extras.

How to read the chapter

Colours
The chapter number sets the colour of all headers. This is chapter @@N@@. Prior knowledge always has its own colour, mustard.

1234567
Contents
Each exercise is a cell in the colour of its type, and each Theory block is one pill that starts with its letter. Click a cell to jump there. A finished exercise gets a green line under its cell.

K12345678910
Links
Almost everything you can click jumps somewhere. A cell in the Contents goes to its exercise or Theory block. A term in a Review or Summary, in the chapter colour, goes to the place where it was introduced. A source line in the Summary goes to its Theory block. The dome at the bottom of the screen goes back to the Contents.
Source tag
A brown label marks a question taken from a source, in that source's wording. A source is on the whole exercise or on its parts, never on both.
Math4AI Ex 2 An arrow in the margin points to a part.
HW1 1aE7 A capsule around a badge marks the whole exercise.
Finished and flag
Two marks sit in the right margin of every exercise. Click the circle when you can do the exercise without help. Click the flag to keep it for the repeat session. Both are saved in this browser and listed at the top of the Contents.
Questions
A question you asked about an exercise is answered right under it, in a Question. box. Click the question to close or open the answer. remove hides it in this browser.
Hints and steps
The grey bar under a question gives the hints: one segment per hint, one hint per click. Inside a solution, why next to a line shows the rule that line uses.
New term
A bold coloured term marks the first use of a new term, explained where it appears.
