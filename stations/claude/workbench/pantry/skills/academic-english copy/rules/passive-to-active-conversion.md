# Converting passive to active

This is a mechanical drill for turning a passive sentence into an active one. For guidance on *when* passive is the right choice, see active-and-passive-voice.md; this file only covers the conversion mechanics.

## Spot the passive verb

A passive verb is a form of "to be" (is, are, was, were, has been, had been) plus a past participle. Often a "by ___" phrase names who or what actually did the action; sometimes that phrase is missing and the actor is only implied.

- *The proposal was rejected by the review board.* -> "by the review board" names the actor.
- *The proposal was rejected.* -> the actor is dropped entirely, but someone still rejected it.

## The conversion move

1. Find the true actor. Check the "by ___" phrase first. If there isn't one, ask who or what logically performed the action.
2. Make that actor the grammatical subject.
3. Rewrite the verb in its active form, matching the actor's tense.

Result: a shorter sentence with a stronger verb, and the sentence usually loses a few words in the process.

- passive: *The window was shattered by the storm.*
- active: *The storm shattered the window.*

## Drill

Work through each pair. The passive line hides or buries the actor; the active line puts it up front.

1. passive: *The laptop was dropped by a delivery worker on the front steps.*
   active: *A delivery worker dropped the laptop on the front steps.*

2. passive: *The coastal road was washed out by last week's flooding.*
   active: *Last week's flooding washed out the coastal road.*

3. passive: *The shared spreadsheet is updated by three different teams every Friday.*
   active: *Three different teams update the shared spreadsheet every Friday.*

4. passive: *The fence post was knocked loose by the neighbor's dog.*
   active: *The neighbor's dog knocked the fence post loose.*

5. passive: *The quarterly report is read by almost no one outside finance.*
   active: *Almost no one outside finance reads the quarterly report.*

6. passive: *The dress code was changed by the school after parents complained.*
   active: *The school changed the dress code after parents complained.*

7. passive: *Her knee was torn by a bad landing during the second match.*
   active: *A bad landing during the second match tore her knee.*

8. passive: *The wildfire was contained before it reached the reservoir, thanks to a shift in wind direction.*
   active: *A shift in wind direction contained the wildfire before it reached the reservoir.*

Note the last one: nothing in the original names an actor directly, but "thanks to" points at one. Passive sentences without any "by" phrase still have an actor hiding somewhere in the sentence, or in context; find it before you convert.

## When the drill breaks down

Some passives resist conversion because no single actor exists. *The bridge was built in 1932* has no useful actor to promote (a construction company, sure, but naming it changes the meaning of the sentence). If the drill produces an active sentence that's clunkier or less accurate than the passive, that's a sign this sentence belongs on the exceptions list in active-and-passive-voice.md, not a sign the drill failed.
