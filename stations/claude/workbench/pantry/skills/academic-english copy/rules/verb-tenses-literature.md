# Verb tenses in literature and humanities writing

## Present tense ("literary present") for:
- Action happening inside the text itself: *In Kafka's The Metamorphosis, Gregor Samsa wakes up to find himself transformed into an insect.*
- Your own readings and other critics' readings of the work: *The whale stands in for forces no captain can master.* / *Bloom argues that Iago's motives stay deliberately unclear.*
- Something that happened earlier than the scene under discussion, marked with **present perfect**: *Nick distrusts Gatsby's story because he has already caught him lying twice.*

## Past tense for:
- Facts about the author's life or the era the work came from: *Woolf wrote much of Mrs Dalloway while recovering from a breakdown.*
- Events that took place before the narrative opens: *...the war that had already claimed her brother.*

## Mixing the two:
A single sentence can pair present tense for what happens in the text with past tense for a fact about the writer's life or history.
- *Toni Morrison, who researched slave narratives extensively [past], builds [present] Beloved's structure around fragmented memory.*
- *Achebe captures [present] the collapse of a village's authority even though he wrote [past] the novel decades after decolonization began.*
