# Stitching words

A stitching word links one sentence or paragraph to the one before it. It tells the reader what kind of move you're making: are you adding another point, contrasting with what you just said, drawing a conclusion? Without one, the reader has to guess the connection on their own.

Repeating a key term from the prior sentence does the same job as a stitching word. Pulling a noun or phrase forward into the new sentence signals that you're still developing the same thread, not starting a new one.

Weak: "Order books fragment liquidity across venues. Retail traders often can't see the best available price."

Better: "Order books fragment liquidity across venues. This fragmentation means retail traders often can't see the best available price."

The second version repeats "fragmentation" and adds "This... means," so the causal link is explicit instead of implied.

## By function

**Similarity**: also, in like manner, in the same way, just as... so too, likewise, similarly

**Exception/Contrast**: although, but, conversely, however, in spite of, on the one hand, on the other hand, nevertheless, nonetheless, notwithstanding, in contrast, on the contrary, still, yet

**Sequence/Order**: first, second, third, next, then, finally

**Time**: after, afterward, at last, before, currently, during, earlier, immediately, later, meanwhile, now, recently, simultaneously, subsequently, then

**Example**: as evidence, for example, for instance, namely, specifically, to illustrate

**Emphasis**: even, indeed, in fact, of course, truly

**Place/Position**: above, adjacent, below, beyond, here, in front, in back, nearby, there

**Additional Support**: additionally, again, also, and, as well, besides, equally important, further, furthermore, in addition, moreover, then

**Cause and Effect**: accordingly, consequently, for this reason, hence, since, so, therefore, thus

**Conclusion/Summary**: finally, in a word, in brief, briefly, in conclusion, in the end, in the final analysis, on the whole, thus, to conclude, to summarize, in sum, to sum up, in summary, in short
