# Verb tenses (general)

**Core rule**: pick one tense logic and hold it. Default to simple present or simple past; reach for other tenses only when the meaning truly needs them.

## Use present tense for
- A claim about your subject matter that holds generally, or a position scholars hold: *Behavioral economists contend that framing effects distort consumer choice.*
- Reporting what a source says, in humanities and social-science writing: *Chomsky argues that syntax is independent of meaning.*
  - Whether the author is alive or dead doesn't matter here. Keep using present tense either way.

## Use past tense for
- Something that happened and stayed in the past, with no bearing on the present as a state of affairs: *The Treaty of Westphalia ended the Thirty Years' War in 1648.*
- A research step you already carried out: *We coded the transcripts between March and May 2021.*

## Mixing present and past in one sentence
A single sentence can hold both: past tense for the specific act of reporting or producing something, present tense for the timeless claim that act delivered.
- *Kahneman and Tversky showed [past] that people weigh [present] losses more heavily than equivalent gains.*
- *The Rosetta Stone is [present] a granodiorite stele. It carries [present] a decree issued [past] by Ptolemy V in 196 BCE.*
