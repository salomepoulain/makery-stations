# Prepositions and prepositional phrases

A preposition links a noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence, marking a relationship in space, time, or logic.

Common prepositions: about, above, across, after, against, along, among, around, at, before, behind, below, beneath, beside, besides, between, beyond, by, concerning, down, during, except, for, from, in, inside, into, like, near, of, off, on, out, outside, over, past, since, through, throughout, to, toward, under, underneath, until, up, upon, with, within, without.

"But" counts as a preposition only when it means "except": *Every station reported except the north gate.*

## Prepositional phrases

A prepositional phrase is a preposition plus its object plus any words modifying that object. The whole phrase acts as either an adjective (modifying a noun) or an adverb (modifying a verb and answering where, when, or how).

- Adverbial: *She left the keys under the doormat.* (tells where she left them)
- Adjectival: *The package under the doormat is mine.* (tells which package)

## Avoid stacking prepositional phrases

Chaining several prepositional phrases together drags a sentence down and buries the main idea. Rework the sentence with an adjective, a possessive, or a compound noun instead.

- Wordy: *the failure of the sensor in the pump at the east station*
- Better: *the east station's pump sensor failure*
