# Parallelism

Put ideas that share a grammatical role into the same grammatical form. This shows up in three places.

## 1. Items joined by a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet)

Every item in a list joined by one of these conjunctions must share the same part of speech and structure. Don't mix a noun with a verb phrase, or a clause with a fragment.
- Bad: *The proposal failed because it lacked funding, ignored community input, and the timeline was unrealistic.*
- Better: *The proposal failed because it lacked funding, ignored community input, and set an unrealistic timeline.*

## 2. Correlative conjunction pairs

Pairs like *not only... but also*, *either... or*, *neither... nor*, and *both... and* each introduce one matching element. Put the conjunction right before the word or phrase it pairs with, not before some other part of the sentence.
- Pairs: *not only... but also; neither... nor; either... or; both... and*
- Bad: *The committee not only reviewed the budget but also the staffing plan.*
- Better: *The committee reviewed not only the budget but also the staffing plan.*

## 3. Comparisons and contrasts

When you compare or contrast two or more things, keep the grammar of each side matched: same part of speech in a list, same tense and mood across clauses.
- Bad: *The lab tested for lead, mercury, and whether arsenic was present.*
- Better: *The lab tested for lead, mercury, and arsenic.* (all nouns)
- Bad: *The intern should have archived the files, and the manager would have signed off on the report.*
- Better: *The intern should have archived the files, and the manager should have signed off on the report.* (consistent mood)
