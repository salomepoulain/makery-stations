# Improving writing style

## 1. Merge short, choppy sentences
A run of short sentences reads like a list, not an argument. Fold minor sentences into the main one with a subordinate clause, a participial phrase, or a compound structure, so the connection between ideas is stated rather than left for the reader to guess.
- Weak: *The committee reviewed the proposal. The proposal lacked a budget. The committee rejected it.*
- Better: *Because the proposal lacked a budget, the committee reviewed and rejected it.*

## 2. Vary sentence openings and structure
Sentence after sentence built as Subject-Verb-Object gets monotonous fast, even when the content is sound. Break the pattern:
- Open with a subordinate clause (*Although the data was incomplete...*, *Once the trial ended...*)
- Open with a participial phrase (*Having reviewed the transcripts, the researchers...*)
- Join two related ideas into one compound-complex sentence instead of stating them separately.

Example: instead of *The results were mixed. The team decided to rerun the experiment.*, write *Because the results were mixed, the team decided to rerun the experiment.*

## 3. Add transitions between sentences and paragraphs
A reader can lose the thread even when every individual sentence is clear, if nothing marks how one point relates to the next. When a draft gets marked "hard to follow" or "disconnected," the fix is usually a missing transition, not a missing idea.

| Relationship | Words to use |
|---|---|
| Similarity | similarly, likewise, in the same way |
| Contrast | however, yet, on the other hand, nevertheless |
| Addition | furthermore, moreover, in addition |
| Cause and effect | therefore, thus, as a result, consequently |
| Time | earlier, afterward, later, meanwhile |
| Summary | in short, overall, to sum up |

At a paragraph break, repeat a key word or phrase from the paragraph that just ended before introducing the new point. That repetition tells the reader the argument is continuing, not restarting.
